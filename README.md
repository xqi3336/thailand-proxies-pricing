## What a Thai IP has to get right

### IP type is the first filter

Thailand is a mobile-first market. Around 50 million people there are online, most of them through phones on 4G and 5G, and Thai shoppers do their browsing inside apps. Platforms built for that traffic have learned to treat raw datacenter ranges as suspicious.

The rough rule: datacenter for Australian retail chains, corporate sites, and anything without anti-bot work; residential for marketplaces and SERPs; mobile when you're testing app behavior, carrier-specific offers, or fighting a bot system that specifically looks for non-cellular patterns. In Thailand, mobile IPs come from AIS, TrueMove H and DTAC; the fixed-line side runs on AIS Fibre, True Online, 3BB, TOT and National Telecom. If a provider's Thai pool leans on one carrier, you'll see that show up as odd results in tests.

### Sessions matter as much as IPs

A rotating Thai IP is fine for reading prices. It breaks anything with a cart, a login, or a multi-step form. What you want there is a sticky session, where the same Thai address holds for a set number of minutes.

"Thailand" as a target is cheap. "Bangkok" is not, at most providers. ## Mistakes that waste Thai proxy budget

**Using datacenter IPs on Thai marketplaces.** The $0.50/GB rate looks appealing until every request lands on a verification page. Datacenter is for soft targets in Thailand, not Lazada.

**Rotating mid-checkout.** Rotating sessions are for list and price pages. Anything with a cart, login, or multi-page form needs a sticky session, or you'll hit account walls built for exactly this pattern.

**Paying the city-targeting multiplier by accident.** If your tooling always sends a `city.` parameter, every gigabyte gets billed at the advanced rate. Drop it for national-level work.

**Assuming one Thai carrier is enough.** AIS, TrueMove H and DTAC behave differently, and provincial fixed-line differs from Bangkok fibre. If your results look uniform across a market where they shouldn't be, carrier concentration is a likely cause.

**Ignoring THB and Thai-language SERPs.** A Thai IP gives you the local experience; it doesn't translate it. Prices come back in baht, and google.co.th returns structurally different results than google.com.
